2. FAMILY A — IMBALANCE-HANDLING GRID (5 classical models x 8 strategies)
   Change being tested: HOW the 578:1 class imbalance is handled.
   Everything else about a model stays identical.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PR-AUC on test set:

  Strategy (what it does)              | LogReg | RF    | XGB   | LGBM  | CatB
  -------------------------------------|--------|-------|-------|-------|------
  raw               (do nothing)       | 0.649  | 0.749 | 0.714 | 0.537 | 0.752
  rus               (delete legit->1:1)| 0.627  | 0.768 | 0.723 | 0.756 | 0.670
  smote             (synthesize->1:1)  | 0.729  | 0.790 | 0.770 | 0.770 | 0.743
  smote_enn         (smote+clean noise)| 0.738  | 0.795*| 0.777 | 0.768 | 0.755
  class_weight      (val-tuned weight) | 0.720  | 0.784 | 0.769 | 0.537=| 0.765
  gan               (GAN synthesis 1:1)| 0.568  | 0.762 | 0.769 | 0.775 | 0.783*

  * = best cell of that strategy.
  CONCLUSIONS:
  A1 Hybrid cleaning (SMOTE-ENN) is the single best classical strategy -> RF
     champion 0.795. Matches Ahmed et al. 2025.
  A2 GAN synthesis is the top strategy for CatBoost (0.783) and rescues
     LightGBM (best LGBM cell); but hurts linear models badly (LogReg 0.568).
     Gains from synthetic data are model-dependent, not universal.
  A3 RUS throws away 99% of data -> weakest overall except for LightGBM.

4. FAMILY C — DEEP ARCHITECTURES (same split/protocol, scaled features)
   Change being tested: network family & sequence context.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Model                          | Extra change      | PR-AUC | F1
  -------------------------------|-------------------|--------|-------
  MLP 128-64-32                  | raw               | 0.775  | 0.809
  MLP                            | pos_weight 518    | 0.747  | 0.763
  MLP                            | SMOTE             | 0.766  | 0.783
  MLP                            | GAN               | 0.719  | 0.750
  AutoEncoder (legit-only recon) | anomaly score     | 0.584  | 0.660
  LSTM 2x64, window=8 txns       | pos_weight        | 0.780  | 0.813
  BiLSTM                         | pos_weight        | 0.781  | 0.839
  LSTM                           | seq-level SMOTE-ENN| 0.758 | 0.736
  LSTM                           | seq-level GAN     | 0.750  | 0.782
  FT-Transformer (3 layers, d=64)| pos_weight        | 0.681  | 0.760
  CONCLUSIONS:
  C1 Temporal windows help: LSTM > plain MLP by +0.005 PR-AUC and better F1.
  C2 Bidirectionality redundant (0.781 vs 0.780): fraud signal is causal.
  C3 Synthetic oversampling HURTS sequences (SMOTE-ENN 0.758 / GAN 0.750 <
     pos_weight 0.780): interpolating temporal windows distorts dynamics.
     Weighting is the right imbalance tool for RNNs.
  C4 Extreme pos_weight hurts NNs too (MLP 0.747 < raw 0.775) - same lesson
     as Family B, independently reproduced.
  C5 FT-Transformer loses to boosting AND to the MLP: attention is not worth
     it on 32 numeric PCA features. Confirms Yu et al. 2024 / KAIS 2026.
  C6 Unsupervised AE trails supervised by ~0.20 PR-AUC despite ROC-AUC 0.94:
     ignoring labels costs ranking precision at the top of the curve.

5b. FAMILY F — GRAPH NEURAL NETWORKS
   Change being tested: does relational structure help when NO entity IDs
   exist? Nodes = transactions; edges = 10 nearest neighbours in standardized
   feature space; one graph per split (inductive, zero cross-split leakage).
   GraphSAGE trained full-batch; GATv2 trained on manually sampled 2-hop
   induced subgraphs (full-batch attention OOMs at ~4M edges on 8GB).
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Model        | Strategy     | PR-AUC | Notes
  -------------|--------------|--------|---------------------------------------
  GraphSAGE    | raw          | 0.488  | saturates to majority under 578:1
  GraphSAGE    | pos_weight   | 0.756  | enters MLP-tier range
  GATv2        | pos_weight   | 0.745  | attention adds cost (775s) not accuracy
  CONCLUSIONS:
  F1 Even a feature-SIMILARITY graph carries signal: SAGE+weight (0.756)
     matches plain MLPs without seeing any sequence context.
  F2 GNNs need class weighting just like every other family.
  F3 Attention (GATv2) buys nothing here and costs 400x SAGE's train time -
     consistent with this dataset having no true relational entities.
  F4 Honest caveat for the report: HGNN papers reporting AUC-PR 0.89 use
     real cardholder/merchant graphs; ULB cannot reproduce that setting.

5c. FAMILY G — OPTUNA HYPERPARAMETER TUNING (TPE sampler, seed=42)
   Change being tested: do tuned hyperparameters beat sensible defaults?
   Objective = validation PR-AUC; boosters tuned on RAW data with
   scale_pos_weight INSIDE the search space (weight learned, not assumed).
   Budgets: RF 25 / XGB 60 / LGBM 60 / CatB 40 / GraphSAGE 25 trials.
   Best params stored in reports/optuna_best_params.json
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Model      | Untuned best -> Tuned (test PR-AUC) | Optuna chose
  -----------|-------------------------------------|---------------------------
  LightGBM   | 0.537 -> 0.753  (+0.216 !!)         | spw=14.9, leaves=90, lr=0.015
  XGBoost    | 0.714(raw) -> 0.769 (+0.055 raw)    | spw=73.5, depth=7, lr=0.055
  RandomFor  | 0.749(raw) -> 0.782 (+0.033 raw)    | depth=32, leaf=2, feat=log2
  CatBoost   | 0.752(raw) -> 0.766 (+0.014 raw)    | spw=4.1 (!), depth=8
  RF+SMOTEENN| 0.795 champion -> 0.794 (tie)       | same RF params transferred
  GraphSAGE  | 0.756 -> 0.751 (-0.005, noise)      | layers=1 (!), k=10, hid=128,
             |                                     | spw=172
  CONCLUSIONS:
  G1 Tuning REPAIRS bad configurations rather than lifting champions:
     LightGBM +0.22, XGB-raw +0.06, RF-raw +0.03 - but every tier champion
     stays within +-0.01. The protocol ceiling on honest time-splits is
     ~0.79-0.80 PR-AUC for this dataset.
  G2 Optuna independently selected MODERATE class weights (4.1 / 14.9 /
     73.5 / 172 - never the naive 518), independently re-discovering the
     Family B lesson through a different mechanism.
  G3 GraphSAGE chose a SINGLE layer: independent confirmation of the
     over-smoothing diagnosis from Family F.
  G4 Validation->test gap remains ~0.09-0.12 across all tuned models =
     temporal drift, not overfitting of the search (EAI ISMLA 2026 reports
     the same effect for chronological validation).

5e. FAMILY I — RANDOM-SPLIT vs TIME-SPLIT ABLATION (6 configs, one per tier)
   Change: stratified shuffled split instead of chronological; everything
   else identical. Output: reports/results_random_split.csv
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Model (best-of-tier)   | TIME  | RANDOM | Inflation from leakage
  -----------------------|-------|--------|-----------------------
  CatBoost + GAN         | 0.780 | 0.840  | +0.060
  MLP + raw              | 0.779 | 0.827  | +0.047
  XGBoost + SMOTE-ENN    | 0.777 | 0.810  | +0.034
  RandomFor + SMOTE-ENN  | 0.795 | 0.812  | +0.017
  LSTM + class_weight    | 0.780 | 0.797  | +0.016
  GraphSAGE + weight     | 0.739 | 0.695  | -0.044 (REVERSED)
  CONCLUSIONS:
  I1 Shuffling inflates 5/6 configs (five-model mean +0.035): future patterns
     leak into training. Measured random numbers (0.80-0.84) move into the band
     the random-split literature reports (0.83-0.88) - most of the historical
     benchmark gap is protocol, not model quality.
  I2 Synthetic-data pipelines are sensitive to split hygiene (CatBoost+GAN,
     +0.060): shuffled synthetic frauds sit next to their real neighbours in
     test.
  I3 GraphSAGE is the exception: shuffling scrambles temporal coherence of
     kNN neighbourhoods, REMOVING signal the time-graph preserved.
  ERRATUM: an initial run cached one GAN generator across both arms, inflating
     the random-side CatBoost+GAN cell to 0.877 / +0.097. gan.py now keys its
     cache on a hash of the training data; this table is from the corrected
     full re-run.

7. PROTOCOL EXPERIMENTS WOVEN THROUGHOUT (methodology decisions tested)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  P1 Threshold tuned on validation max-F1, frozen before test  -> prevents
     test leakage; thresholds in ledger range 0.07-1.0 showing why this matters
  P2 Time-based vs random split: literature (EAI ISMLA 2026) confirms
     chronological validation yields LOWER PR-AUC than random stratified;
     our 0.795 sits at top of honest-protocol range (~0.75-0.80) while
     random-split papers report 0.83-0.88 on identical dataset
  P3 GPU acceleration verified: torch cu130 on RTX 5050 (sm_120);
     XGBoost/CatBoost device=cuda, LSTM batches on GPU
  P4 Seeds fixed at 42 (plus 5-seed sweep for ensemble D)
